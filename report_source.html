Part 1 · Application and design

Workflow, interfaces and the four screens

The operations analyst forecasts rentals during [t, t+1 hour). A completed hour’s count and weather are assumed available immediately when that hour ends. This is a system-wide historical replay, not a live station-allocation application.

The four screens

  • Data overview: browse source rows and missing timestamps for a chosen range, inspect period audits, hourly/day-type rental patterns, and the fixed Training/Validation count distributions.
  • Forecast one hour: select an observed Test hour and saved model, press Forecast, inspect the predicted count, available intervals and input-source times, then reveal the actual count separately. Changing selection clears the previous output.
  • Compare models: inspect Validation selection and fixed full-Test scores for all five methods, choose a model/date range for an actual-versus-forecast plot, and download the displayed rows and full score table.
  • Inspect model behaviour: examine Lasso penalty/coefficient paths, the calendar/full-input Ridge experiment, grouped interval coverage/width, and the default model’s largest errors and hourly residuals.
InterfaceInputs → outputs / responsibility
data.read_and_auditCSV → valid chronological observations, audit, missing times, exclusions.
features.build_features; PreprocessorKnown target calendar + completed history → raw features, flags and frozen 59-column matrix.
models.predictLoaded method and predictors → raw/scored means and available distributions.
forecast_hour; reveal_outcomeEarlier records/calendar → forecast; a separate function reads the target outcome.
train_evaluate.run_pipelineTraining fits → Validation choices → freeze → Test evaluation and saved results.
app.py; screens/Cached loading, navigation, readable evidence and CSV exports.

Two implemented design choices

Shared prediction logic: training/evaluation and the app reuse features.py, models.py and forecasting.py instead of implementing a second screen-specific predictor. Forecast applies loaded fitted objects; CSVs supply an agreement reference, not the prediction calculation.

Exact-time history and frozen preprocessing: timestamp lookup replaces row shifting because adjacent rows may not be consecutive hours. Training-only medians/scales/categories replace fitting transformations on the selected range, preventing leakage and browsing-dependent forecasts.

Before the app: audit → construct → fit Training → select Validation → freeze → save/evaluate Test. In the app: select → Forecast → inspect → Reveal. No model retraining or conversational-agent call occurs during a forecast. Framework: Streamlit [6].

Part 2 · Data and modelling

Five methods, selected settings and grid limits

Seasonal baseline: frozen Training mean for (hour, workingday), with overall Training mean as fallback. Fractional means are retained; no required distribution is supplied.

Ridge: squared error plus λ‖w‖²; Lasso: (1/2n) squared error plus λ‖w‖₁. Both use b+xᵀw and an unpenalized scikit-learn intercept [2] [3]. Penalty scaling differs between methods; equal alpha values are not equivalent strengths.

Poisson: μ=exp(b+xᵀw), fitted with an L2 penalty [4]. Y|x is Poisson(μ), with variance μ. Finite coefficients/convergence and varying predictions are checked. Its count intervals treat μ as fixed and omit coefficient-estimation uncertainty.

Bayesian: β∼N(0,I/α), y|β∼N(Φβ,σ²I), including the intercept in the prior. Linear solves obtain m and S; future mean is φᵀm and variance σ²+φᵀSφ. This Gaussian approximation is not a nonnegative count law.

Five-model comparison and probability score

All methods use identical observed Test targets. MAE averages absolute actual-minus-scored errors; RMSE is the square root of mean squared errors and emphasizes large misses. Units are rentals per hour. Ridge remains default because of Validation selection, independent of subsequent Test ranking.

Ridge reduces Test MAE from 115.8183 to 48.9561 (57.73%). Test error is slightly below Validation’s 50.2291, but the periods differ and this is not proof of a general temporal improvement. Lasso and Bayesian are only 0.0051 and 0.0067 MAE worse on Test; no uncertainty analysis establishes that these differences are practically meaningful.

Poisson improves MAE over the baseline but has worse RMSE: 174.7885 versus 162.5811. Large misses dominate its squared loss; at 2012-09-11 18:00 it predicts 2,532.9426 versus 877 actual. An exponential link can amplify a large linear predictor, but this case alone does not identify a causal input.

Poisson mean negative log count probability = mean[−logpmf(y, μ)] = 25.721577 nats/hour.

The stable discrete log probability is used without rounding μ or forming factorials [5]. Lower is better; this is not a Gaussian density score. Fit times measure the selected candidate fit only, excluding preprocessing, search, prediction and scoring. They are not end-to-end runtime benchmarks.

Negative Gaussian lower bounds: 903 of 3,632 nominal 95% Bayesian intervals (24.86%) have a negative lower endpoint; 800 of these occur at hours 00:00–06:00. At 80%, the count is 766 (21.09%). Counts cannot be negative, so this exposes the Gaussian approximation’s weak support at low demand. Original endpoints remain visible and enter the coverage/width audit unchanged; truncating them would conceal that limitation.

σ² was selected using Validation MAE. This rewards mean accuracy, not coverage: narrow intervals are not penalized. The selected .5 multiplier reduces the noise variance relative to its Training estimate and contributes to narrow ranges. Poisson equidispersion and Bayesian constant-noise assumptions also limit calibration. Neither model is recalibrated on Test.

Coverage and width must be read together. Bayesian ranges are wider and cover more outcomes, but still miss nominal targets. Nearly constant widths reflect dominant constant observation noise. Group statistics use their own n; a low n warrants caution.

1 · 2012-10-02 08:00 — overprediction

Actual 134; forecast 547.8037; error −413.8037. This Tuesday working hour follows 260 at 07:00, with day/week lags 784/805. Usual busy-hour and historical signals point well above the actual, which drops below even the preceding count. The data do not identify an external cause. An earlier-validated risk flag based on available lag disagreement could communicate uncertainty rather than claim an observable cause.

2 · 2012-09-25 17:00 — missed demand ramp

Actual 967; forecast 576.1750; error +390.8250. The Tuesday working hour rises from 495 at 16:00. Prior-day same-hour count is 898 but prior-week is 338. The forecast lies between conflicting historical signals and misses the jump. The +36.35 mean Test residual at 17:00 supports investigating a recurring peak limitation. A calendar-dependent response to history is a hypothesis for future evaluation, not a proved cause.

3 · 2012-09-08 16:00 — abrupt low outcome

Actual 114; forecast 495.6976; error −381.6976. This Saturday non-working hour has previous count 512 and day/week lags 470/462. All point much higher than actual. Prior humidity is .88, but that observation does not establish that humidity caused the fall; target-hour weather is withheld. Permitted past inputs cannot anticipate every abrupt external change. Better risk communication or validated uncertainty may be more useful than a causal story unsupported by data.

These hypotheses were motivated by Test inspection and remain unimplemented. Future revisions need a genuinely untouched later assessment period. Reusing the current Test to select and report improvements would invalidate the holdout claim. The in-memory grid probe used only Training and Validation and did not replace the frozen submission.

Executed correctness evidence

The final submission rerun passed all 17 checks with no failures/errors/skips in 8.45 seconds. An initial rerun failed fixture setup because the temporary parent directory was absent; creating it resolved that environmental issue without skipping or changing tests. Earlier visual-update checks also passed. The test sources and outcomes are retained.

  • Complete counts 1…24 give mean 12.5; removing a required timestamp makes the mean missing. Observed zero stays zero with a false flag.
  • Changing target/future count/weather leaves current inputs and predictions unchanged; eligible earlier count changes update the correct lag.
  • Changing Test values and training into an isolated directory leaves Training transforms, selected settings, fitted-model hashes and default unchanged.
  • MAE=2 and RMSE=√(14/3) on the independent three-row fixture; inclusive coverage=2/3; widths are checked separately.
  • Poisson mean 100 has 80% endpoints 87/113 and 95% endpoints 81/120. The Bayesian toy gives m=(1.6,1.2), coefficient variance 2 and total variance 3.
  • Covariance symmetry, nonnegative variance, serialization/replay agreement, invalid selection messages, Reveal/reset, browser layouts and CSV content agreement were checked.

Remaining assumptions include missing external drivers, historical distribution shift, Gaussian support below zero, fixed noise/count laws and system-wide aggregation. Correct arithmetic alone does not establish reliable uncertainty.
